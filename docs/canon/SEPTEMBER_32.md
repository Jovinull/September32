# September 32

**Status: mostly IDEA. Read `docs/canon/README.md` before adding anything here.**

## What is observed — `PROVISIONAL`

These are things the archive contains. They are observations, not explanations.

- A class photograph, unlabelled, out of sequence, stamped with a September date
  ending in 32.
- An attendance ledger column, ruled and filled in the same hand as the columns
  around it, with **no date written above it**. Fourteen entries.
- Later, and not yet written: letters, a tape, catalogued lost property, and a
  run of the student paper with a gap nobody can account for.

Some records contradict others. Some people remember incompatible things. Both
of those are more important than the date itself.

## What is NOT decided — `IDEA`

**The cause is undecided and does not need deciding yet.** Every hypothesis
below is `IDEA`. None may be relied on by any scene.

- *A clerical artefact.* Somebody once needed a day that did not exist, and the
  institution has been quietly maintaining the fiction ever since.
- *A local phenomenon.* The town has a day the rest of the calendar does not,
  and the school is simply the thing in town that keeps the best records.
- *A perceptual phenomenon.* Nothing extra happened; some people experienced a
  day twice, and the archive is faithfully recording that.
- *Something the archive does.* Cataloguing an event changes it. Iris's habit of
  photographing objects before cataloguing them stops being a quirk.

They are not equally interesting, and they are not mutually exclusive. Recording
them is not a shortlist to pick from; it is a record of what has been thought
about, so the eventual answer can be checked against all of it.

## Why this is deliberately open

The first phase of the game is people living ordinary lives, and none of that
writing depends on the answer. What it depends on is `PHENOMENON_RULES.md` — the
constraints. An explanation chosen now would leak into scenes written now, and
the leak would be invisible until it was expensive.

## The three questions the ending must answer — `PROVISIONAL`

Whatever the explanation turns out to be, it has to answer these, because the
game will have spent hours making the player care about them:

1. **Why the records and not the memories?** The archive is more reliable than
   the people, which is the wrong way round and is the actual horror.
2. **Who benefits from the date being maintained?** Somebody has been keeping
   this filed for a long time.
3. **What does it cost to be the one who knows?** The protagonist ends the game
   holding something. The ending is about what they do with it.
