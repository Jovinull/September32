# The protagonist

**Status:** `PROVISIONAL`
**Speaks as:** `c_me`. **Narrates as:** `narrator`.

Every other character in this project has a bible. The person whose voice the
player reads for the entire game did not, until Milestone 4 — which is how a
cast of five got carefully differentiated while the sixth voice, the one
present in every single line of narration, was written by instinct.

## Function

Not a blank, and not a character with a backstory. Someone **specific enough to
be surprised by** — which is the open question `OPEN_QUESTIONS.md` records, and
this file describes where the writing currently sits rather than settling it.

They got the job because nobody else wanted it. That is the whole of their
claim on the story and it should stay that way: the protagonist is not special,
and the game is not about them becoming special.

## Default worldview

**Notices process.** Their eye goes to how a thing is organised, who maintains
it, and what the arrangement implies about somebody. A rota is more interesting
to them than a rumour. This is why the archive job suits them and why they were
given it.

**Assumes the boring explanation, and keeps assuming it slightly past the point
where it is comfortable.** They are not a sceptic — June is the sceptic. They
are somebody who would rather not be the person who got excited.

**Is new, and is aware of being new.** A recurring, deliberate note: they
register that other people have a history here that they are outside of, and
they are neither wounded nor entitled about it.

## Narration style

**Close third-person-ish second person.** "You put it back in the drawer."

**Reports, then declines to interpret.** The single most important rule in the
project's style, and it belongs to this voice more than anyone's: the narration
says what happened and does not say what it meant. `docs/style/WRITING_STYLE.md`
is the authority; the failure mode is a clause beginning *which is* that tells
the reader how to feel.

**Specific numbers, casually.** Eleven minutes. Four inches. Ninety seconds.
Nineteen pages. The precision is characterisation — this is a person who counts
things without deciding to — and it is the closest thing the narration has to a
tic. Use it. Do not use it in every paragraph.

**Notices its own behaviour, one beat late.** "You are aware that you put it
back rather than filing it, and that those are different." This is the voice's
main comic and emotional register at once, and it should arrive as a
self-observation rather than as a confession.

**Funnier in narration than in dialogue.** Out loud they are dry and brief. On
the page they are wry. That gap is deliberate and is most of the characterisation
there is.

## Dialogue style

**Short.** Usually one line. Often four words.

**Answers the question asked.** They do not deflect like June or omit like Mara;
if they are not going to say something they say nothing, which is a different
move and is available as a menu option.

**Does not perform.** No speeches. No summarising what a scene meant. If a scene
ends on a line of theirs it is a small one.

> `c_me "That's not my name."`
>
> `c_me "It's a tin."`
>
> `c_me "I'm ahead of schedule."`

## What traits change, and what they do not

Traits accumulate; they do not fork the character.

**Traits may change:**

- which options are on a menu (`has_trait("curious")` gates a few)
- how a line is phrased inside a branch
- what the narration chooses to notice first

**Traits may NOT change:**

- the narrating voice's rhythm, register or humour
- whether the protagonist is decent (they are; the range is between kinds of
  decent and one or two real failures of nerve)
- whether they count things
- the fact that they are new

A `direct` protagonist and a `reserved` protagonist should be recognisably the
same person having a different week. If a reader could believe they were two
people, the trait has been written as a character rather than as a lean.

The eight traits, and what leaning on one looks like:

| Trait | The lean |
| --- | --- |
| `curious` | Asks the follow-up. Stays four minutes longer. |
| `gentle` | Notices the cost to the other person and pays part of it. |
| `direct` | Says the thing. Sometimes at a price. |
| `reserved` | Chooses not to fill a silence, and the silence works. |
| `skeptical` | Wants the dull explanation first. Sides with June more often than not. |
| `empathetic` | Reads the room and is occasionally wrong about it. |
| `sarcastic` | The one that can actually cost something. Used sparingly and never for free. |
| `impulsive` | Currently unwritten — no Act 1 branch produces it. |

## Boundaries

**Never** narrates a feeling the player has not been given cause for.

**Never** tells the player something is wrong. The prose reports; the player
notices. This is canon in `THEMES.md` and it is enforced by reading, not by a
tool.

**Never** knows something the state does not hold. Every callback is guarded.

**Never** becomes the person who solves it. Whatever September 32 turns out to
be, the protagonist's part is what they do with what they know, not being the
one who was always meant to find it.

## Background currently canon

Almost none, deliberately.

- Arrived on a Sunday, alone, after everybody else had arrived on the Friday.
- Assigned the archive digitisation because nobody wanted it.
- Has four shirts and a bag of laundry they have been meaning to take into town.
- Has not been named, and is not gendered anywhere in the prose.

Everything else is `OPEN QUESTION`. Where they are from, why they transferred,
and whether they chose this school are all unwritten, and no scene may imply an
answer.
