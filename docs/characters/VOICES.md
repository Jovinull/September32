# Speech fingerprints

A working reference for writing dialogue. The full bibles are per-character; this
is the page you keep open while drafting.

**The test.** Cover the names on three consecutive lines. A reader should be able
to say who spoke them. Not from a catchphrase — from rhythm, from what the line
declines to do.

**The anti-test.** If a character has a tic in every line, that is a gimmick, not
a voice. The fingerprint should be detectable across a scene, not stamped on
every sentence.

---

## The one-line summary

| | Talks like |
| --- | --- |
| **Iris** | Two clauses too long, then a short one that lands. Ends conversations by going back to work. |
| **Theo** | Full stops where others use commas. Starts mid-thought. Produces documents instead of arguing. |
| **Mara** | Flat declaratives and a pause you are meant to notice. Says "here" instead of naming the place. |
| **June** | Fluent, quick, finished sentences. Answers the easy half of a question and moves on. |
| **Noah** | Talks through the gap where you would have replied. Writes better than he speaks. |

## June

**Rhythm.** Fast, fluent, complete. Her sentences arrive already finished — no
trailing off, no visible searching. Two or three short sentences in a row, then
a longer one that changes the subject.

**Contractions.** Freely, more than anyone else in the cast. "I'd rather not",
"that's the thing", "you don't have to".

**How she evades.** She answers the *easy half* of a question, warmly and fully,
and then asks you something. It does not read as evasion. It reads as interest,
which is why it works.

**How she lies.** Rarely, and badly, by over-specifying. A true June answer is
short. A false one acquires detail.

**Humour.** First to joke, generous, usually at her own expense. **The tell: a
joke that arrives too fast, before the other person has finished.**

**What she notices.** People's comfort. Who has gone quiet. Who is being left
out. She is the first to register a mood and the last to name it.

**What she ignores.** Anything institutional — dates, records, the noticeboard.
Not from stupidity; from a settled decision that it is not interesting.

**What embarrasses her.** Being thanked sincerely. She will deflect it with a
joke inside two seconds.

**What makes her angry.** Being told she is in denial. She agrees pleasantly,
finishes the conversation properly, and does not come back.

**Under stress.** Funnier and faster. The jokes get closer together.

**Never.** A long sombre speech. Trailing off. Saying "I suppose you're right."

> "You don't have to help. I'm saying that as a formality, obviously, I've
> already decided you're helping."

## Mara

**Rhythm.** Flat declaratives, mostly short. She uses pauses the way other
people use adjectives — she will stop exactly long enough for you to notice,
then continue as though she had not.

**Vocabulary.** Plain, never ornamental. **Says "here" where others would name
the town.** Uses people's full names when she is being careful.

**How she evades.** She states a fact adjacent to the question and stops. Not a
deflection — an offer. You can take the adjacent fact or you can push.

**How she lies.** She does not, quite. She omits, and she will tell you she is
omitting: "There's a version of that. I'm not going to tell it well."

**Humour.** Very dry, easy to miss, almost always about the town. Delivered
without any signal that it was a joke.

**What she notices.** Who is talking to whom, and who stopped. She has a map of
the school's social weather and it is usually accurate.

**Her competence has a cost, and it is this:** she trusts the map. When it is
wrong, she is *confidently* wrong, and being contradicted about people is the
thing she takes worst.

**What she ignores.** Documents. Anything written down. Her version of the town
is oral and she has never had cause to check it.

**What embarrasses her.** Being caught being kind.

**What makes her angry.** An outsider being clever about the town. She goes
quiet, and the quiet is the last warning.

**Under stress.** Shorter sentences, longer pauses.

**Never.** Gossip for its own sake. Superstition. Being rustic or quaint.

> "That's not what happened."
>
> "..."
>
> "It's what people say happened, which is different, and I'd rather you had
> both."

## Noah

**Rhythm.** Fast and forward. **He talks through the gap where you would have
answered** — not rudely; he simply has the next sentence ready. Prone to a
prepared phrase he has clearly rehearsed on the walk over.

**Vocabulary.** Journalistic and slightly overreached for a four-page school
paper. "Sources." "The piece." "We're running it." The *we* is him.

**How he evades.** He does not. He tells you exactly what he wants and what he
will do with it. **That is what makes him dangerous** — the player keeps waiting
for the catch.

**How he lies.** By omission of scale. He will say "someone mentioned it" when
it was one person, once, in passing.

**Humour.** Frequent, generous, occasionally at someone's expense — and then a
real apology, immediately, which is one of the better things about him.

**What he notices.** Whether a thing is *printable*. He sorts the world into
material and not-material, continuously, and does not hide it.

**What he ignores.** Whether people want to be written about. Genuinely does not
occur to him as a separate question from whether it is true.

**What embarrasses him.** The size of the paper. Do not mention the circulation.

**What makes him angry.** Being told a story is not his to tell.

**Under stress.** Talks more.

**The three readings must all stay viable.** If Noah is calm about something
strange, the scene must leave open: he knows something / he does not care /
you are reading too much into him. Never resolve it in the prose.

**Never.** Be a schemer. Hint. Withhold for effect. His danger is sincerity.

> "I'm not being coy, I'll just tell you: I want twenty minutes in that room
> with a camera. You can say no. I'll ask again on Thursday."

## Shared prohibitions

Nobody in this cast says:

- "I suppose you're right."
- "Perhaps."
- "For some reason, I couldn't shake the feeling..."
- "A chill ran down my spine."
- "Something about her expression seemed different."
- "Maybe it was nothing."

Nobody states their own subtext. Nobody explains a joke. Nobody speaks in
paragraphs unless the length itself is the point.

Not every scene ends on an ominous line. Most should not.
