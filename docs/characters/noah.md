# Noah

**Status:** `PROVISIONAL`
**Data file:** `data/characters/noah.json`

## Function

**Role**
Student. Runs the school paper, which is four pages and largely him.

**Narrative function**
The pressure to *publish*. Not cynical: he genuinely believes the truth should be
printed, and that belief is its own kind of problem. He is how the story asks
what happens when the archive stops being private.

## Interior

**Public personality**
Enthusiastic, talkative, slightly too pleased with a good line. Persistent in a
way that reads as charm right up until it does not.

**Private personality**
He knows the paper is small and he is doing it anyway, which takes a stubbornness
he would not describe as such.

**Core desire.** To have printed something that mattered.
**Core fear.** That nothing here does.
**Core wound.** Not decided. `IDEA`
**Core contradiction.** He believes the truth belongs to everyone, and he wants
to be the one who tells it.

**The lie he tells himself.** That publishing is the same thing as helping.
**What he actually needs.** To find out that some things are better held.

**Values.** Publication. Not sitting on things. Credit, if he is honest.
**Boundaries.** Being told a story is not his to tell.

## Voice

**Speech patterns.** Fast, enthusiastic, keeps talking through the pause where
you would have answered. Writes better than he speaks, and knows it.

**Vocabulary.** Journalistic and slightly overreached. "Sources", "the piece",
"we're running it".

**Humour.** Frequent, generous, occasionally at someone's expense and then
apologetic.

**Topics avoided.** Almost none, which is the problem.

**Under stress.** Talks more.
**When comfortable.** Talks more.

## Relationships

**Noah → player.** Moves on `familiarity` immediately. Easy to know, hard to
trust with anything.

**Noah → Iris.** Wants access to the archive. She has not said no; she has said
"not yet", four times.

**Noah → Theo.** Wants his ledger findings. Theo will not hand them over without a
standard of proof Noah does not have.

## Knowledge

**Knows.** That something is going on in the archive, in outline, without detail.
**Does not know.** Any of the specifics.
**Suspects.** That he is being managed. Correct.
**False beliefs.** That printing it would settle it.

**Secrets.** Not really a secret-keeper. That is the point of him.

## Arc

**Arc possibilities.** From "print it" to understanding what printing costs. Or:
he prints it, is right to, and it goes badly anyway.

**Romantic.** Not flagged as romanceable. He is more useful as the friend whose
interests genuinely conflict with the player's.

**Friendship.** Real, warm, and a liability.

**Antagonistic.** The most likely antagonist in the cast, and never a villain. He
publishes. That is all it takes.

## Constraints

**Never do.** Lie about what he will do with information. He tells you.
**Never say.** Anything that makes him a schemer. His danger is sincerity.

## Writing constraints

**Speech rhythm.** Fast and forward. He talks through the gap where you would
have answered — not rudely; he has the next sentence ready. Prone to a phrase he
rehearsed on the walk over.

**Vocabulary.** Journalistic, slightly overreached for four pages. "Sources."
"The piece." "We're running it." The *we* is him.

**How he evades.** He does not. He tells you what he wants and what he will do
with it. That is what makes him dangerous: the player keeps waiting for a catch
that is not coming.

**How he lies.** By omission of scale. "Someone mentioned it" when it was one
person, once, in passing.

**What he notices.** Whether a thing is printable. He sorts the world into
material and not-material, continuously, and does not hide it.

**What he ignores.** Whether people want to be written about. It does not occur
to him as a question separate from whether it is true.

**What embarrasses him.** The size of the paper. Do not mention the circulation.

**What makes him angry.** Being told a story is not his to tell.

**Under stress.** Talks more.

## The three readings

**This is the hardest constraint in the cast, and it is not optional.**

When Noah is calm about something strange, a player must be able to come away
with any of:

1. Noah knows something.
2. Noah does not care.
3. The protagonist is reading too much into him.

All three stay viable until the story deliberately closes one. A scene that
resolves it — a knowing pause, a line the narration flags as significant, a beat
held a fraction too long — has spent something the game has not earned yet.

**The failure mode to avoid** is "mysterious boy who obviously knows". It would
make the central mystery predictable, and it is what every draft will reach for.

**The correction.** Whenever Noah is unbothered, the scene must supply an
*ordinary* reason he might be: he is thinking about the layout, he has heard
four rumours this month, he genuinely finds the archive boring. The ordinary
reason must be as available as the sinister one, and the prose must not choose.
