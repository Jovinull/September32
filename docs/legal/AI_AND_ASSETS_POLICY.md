# AI and third-party assets

**Status: binding policy.** Stricter than most of the licences require, on
purpose.

## The short version

1. AI may be used freely to build this project.
2. The shipped game contains no AI at all.
3. No third-party asset is ever sent to an AI service, for any reason.
4. No third-party asset is ever modified by a generative model.
5. An artist's stated wishes about AI are respected even where a licence would
   permit ignoring them.

## 1. AI in development — permitted

Code, research, tooling, drafting, continuity checking, review, documentation.
This project was bootstrapped with heavy AI assistance and that is recorded
plainly rather than hidden.

## 2. AI in the shipped game — prohibited

See `docs/decisions/ADR-0004-no-runtime-ai.md`. No model, no API, no generated
text or images, no network access of any kind. Enforced by tests that walk the
engine package and fail on a non-standard-library import or a networking module.

## 3. Third-party assets are never sent to an AI service

**Not for generation. Not for editing. Not for analysis. Not for "just looking at
it".**

This is stricter than most licences require, and the reason is that the
alternative is a judgement call made repeatedly by different people under time
pressure. A rule with no exceptions is enforceable; a rule with a "reasonable
use" carve-out is not.

The visible consequence is in the character audit. `s32 assets audit` measures
canvas size, framing, head-to-body proportion, line weight and colour statistics
— all computed locally from pixel data by `tools/s32tools/assets/audit.py`.
Nothing is uploaded. The report says so, and states plainly what the method
*cannot* tell you: whether two characters look like they belong in the same
school. That question is left to a human, and it is recorded as open in
`docs/canon/OPEN_QUESTIONS.md` rather than answered by an upload.

Contact sheets (`s32 assets sheets`) exist for the same reason: a human can look
at four hundred sprites at once, locally, without any of them leaving the
machine.

## 4. No generative modification

The image pipeline (`tools/s32tools/assets/process.py`) does greyscale, resize,
brightness, contrast, saturation and a tint blend. That is the entire list. Every
operation is arithmetic a person could describe in one sentence.

Never: upscaling by model, inpainting, outpainting, style transfer, img2img,
"AI retouch", background removal by model, or a generative fill.

There is also a limit on what non-generative processing is *for*. Grading can
pull two compatible packs into the same room. It cannot make two different art
styles look like one, and pushing it far enough to try produces something that
looks processed — which is worse than the mismatch. When the audit says a pack
does not fit, the answer is fewer, more coherent assets.

## 5. No AI-generated assets in the project

Packs described as Stable Diffusion, Midjourney, "AI generated" or "AI assisted"
are not used for characters, backgrounds, or anything else that ships.

Two reasons, and the first is the practical one: the provenance and licensing of
generated imagery is unsettled, varies by jurisdiction, and is not a risk worth
taking with a project's entire visual identity. The second is that the game
should not look like an asset dump.

All assets currently installed carry an explicit statement from their creator
that no generative AI was used, or are CC0 from a creator who publishes their
process. Recorded per-entry in `assets/catalog/assets.toml`.

## 6. Respect an artist's stated wishes

**If a page says CC0 and also says "do not use with AI", use a different asset.**

CC0 would technically permit ignoring the request. This project does not, and
will not entertain the argument that it may. The cost of finding another asset is
an afternoon. The cost of being the project that argued with an artist about
their own work is not recoverable.

### Recorded exclusion: NoranekoGames

Not used in this bootstrap. Their terms include a broad restriction against use
"with AI in any way", with language resembling "paired with". This project uses
AI heavily during development, and rather than construct an argument about
whether writing code with AI assistance counts as pairing an asset with AI, the
assets are simply not used.

That is not a criticism of those terms. It is what a broad restriction is *for*:
it tells a developer to go elsewhere, and this one did.

## 7. What is recorded

Every asset carries an `ai_related_terms` field in the manifest, quoting what the
source page said and when it was read, plus the evidence file in
`assets/evidence/<id>/`. `s32 assets verify` flags any entry whose AI terms
mention a prohibition, so it gets a human decision rather than a default.

## 8. Disclosure

The generated `CREDITS.md` states that no asset in this project was generated by
AI and that no third-party asset was sent to an AI service. That statement is
true, is generated from the manifest, and is checked by CI.

Whether and how to disclose AI's role in *development* on a store page is an open
question — `docs/canon/OPEN_QUESTIONS.md`. The honest default is to say so.
