# Act 1 — the first week

**Status: `ACT1_DRAFT`.** The shape is decided. Nothing here settles canon; where
Act 1 needed an answer the project has not given, it is marked `OPEN QUESTION`
and the scene was written so as not to need one.

Seven narrative days. Not seven chapters — the architecture is still storylets,
and two players' weeks should differ substantially in who they spent time with,
what they learned, what they promised and which reading of the evidence they
lean toward. What they share is five anchors and a question.

## Purpose

Answer, for the first time, whether September 32 is **interesting to play for a
week** rather than interesting to have built.

Concretely, by the end of Act 1 the player should:

- know what an ordinary day here is, well enough to notice one that is not;
- have a preference between five people, and have paid something for it;
- hold two records that do not agree, and no explanation that covers both;
- not know what September 32 is, and not feel cheated about that.

## Emotional progression

| Day | Beat | What the day is for |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 1 | `ARRIVAL` | Orientation. Places, faces, the job. Almost no mystery. |
| 2 | `ROUTINE` | What a normal day is. Timetable, hatch, corridor, dorm, the road. |
| 3 | `CONNECTION` | People start being specific. First real group dynamic. |
| 4 | `INCONSISTENCY` | One thing does not fit, and has an ordinary explanation. |
| 5 | `DOUBT` | Two people read the same evidence differently, both reasonably. |
| 6 | `CONSEQUENCE` | Something the player did earlier changes the day. |
| 7 | `CONTRADICTION` | A second record. The ordinary explanations stop covering both. |

The days are a **dramatic model, not a schedule**. A run that spends day four in
the courtyard and never opens a ledger is a legitimate run; the beat arrives when
its anchor's requirements are met, which is what anchors are for. Days 5 and 6
have no anchor at all — they are shapes that emerge from storylets whose
requirements only a few days of play can satisfy.

## Anchors

Five. Anchors are the only mandatory content, and their requirements name
*knowledge and state*, never dates alone.

| Order | Anchor | Earliest | Phase after | Beat |
| ---: | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `arrival` | day 1 | `normal` | Arriving at the gate on a Sunday. |
| 2 | `first_shift` | day 1 | `normal` | Iris explains the job. |
| 3 | `the_common_room` | day 3 | `normal` | Four people in one room, and the player is not the subject. |
| 4 | `the_undated_column` | day 4 | **`odd`** | An attendance column, ruled and filled, with no date above it. |
| 5 | `the_date` | day 6 | `odd` | The photograph's stamp. Two records that do not agree. |

Two changes from the Milestone 2 chain, both deliberate:

**`the_undated_column` now enters `odd`, not `the_date`.** The phase model says
`odd` is "one thing does not fit, singular, and it has an ordinary explanation".
That is the ledger column exactly — somebody forgot to write the date, which is
what everyone says and what is probably true. The photograph stamped `32` is a
much stronger thing and was doing the weaker job.

**`the_date` no longer advances the phase.** Act 1 ends *inside* `odd`, holding
two records and no account that covers both. `unsettling` is the second and third
thing, and it belongs to Act 2. The week ends with

> "This is no longer just a clerical mistake."

and not with

> "I now understand what September 32 is."

## What the player may learn, and what they may not

**Available in Act 1**

- The archive holds an unlabelled class photograph, out of sequence.
- Its stamp ends in a number that is not a September date.
- Ledger III has a column with entries and no date. Fourteen names.
- Iris keeps a second, private index, and it does not match the accession record.
- Bound volumes of the paper were moved to the annexe during a reorganisation.
- The town's account of its own history skips a year, told third-hand.
- Theo has re-checked the crate twice and lost a brass key.

**Not available in Act 1, and no scene may imply it**

- What September 32 is. Any of the four hypotheses in `SEPTEMBER_32.md`.
- Whether anyone alive was present for it.
- Who has been maintaining the record, or that anyone has.
- Anything about Ms. Ellery beyond a person with a filing system and a job.
- Any of the five characters' `core wound`s, all of which are still `IDEA`.

The last one is the constraint that shaped most of the writing. Every character
has a dramatic question this week; none of them has an answer.

## Character questions for the week

Not routes. A question the player can watch someone carry, and can affect
without resolving.

### Iris — *what does she want somebody else to see?*

She has been alone with the job since March and has stopped noticing. The second
index is the thing she has never described to anyone as anything but a quirk.
What she needs is not to be believed; it is to be **accompanied**.

The player can reach the shoebox. They cannot reach why it matters to her.

**Non-mystery scene:** `iris_outside` — she eats lunch at eleven so she can be
alone at one, on a wall in the middle of the courtyard, which is not where people
who want to be alone sit.

### Mara — *what is she not saying about the town?*

She would defend this place to anyone and has been counting down to leaving since
she was fourteen. The player is the first outsider in a while who might
understand it correctly, which is both what she wants and what she is afraid of.

**Non-mystery scene:** `mara_the_long_way` — she walks the player home the long
way for no stated reason, and the scene is about a dog, a gate, and how long it
takes to stop being new somewhere.

### Theo — *why does he need the crate to be complete?*

Checking is how he cares. He has re-checked things for people who never knew.
What he needs is to be wrong about something small, safely, in front of someone
who does not make it a lesson — and Act 1 gives him exactly one chance at that.

**Non-mystery scene:** `theo_the_spreadsheet` — his spreadsheet has a column
nobody asked for, and the scene is about being pleased with something small.

### June — *why does she need this place to be ordinary?*

She wants it to be nothing, urgently, and the urgency is not about the archive.
She is the sceptical reading given good arguments, and rule R5 says the player
must never feel stupid for agreeing with her.

**Non-mystery scene:** `june_the_kitchen` — the kitchen rota, a birthday nobody
organised, and June organising it in about four minutes.

### Noah — *what does he think printing something would fix?*

He believes the truth belongs to everyone and wants to be the one who tells it.
Act 1's job is to make the player like him before that becomes a problem.

**Non-mystery scene:** `noah_the_sports_report` — he has to file 200 words on a
fixture he did not watch, and would rather do it properly.

## Relationship opportunities introduced

Now that direction is explicit
([`RELATIONSHIP_DIRECTION.md`](RELATIONSHIP_DIRECTION.md)), Act 1 uses all three
kinds:

- **`x->player`** — the ordinary case, and most of the week.
- **`player->x`** — rarer and deliberate. `june_the_kitchen` and
  `mara_the_long_way` each contain a moment where the *player's* view of someone
  changes, and that is what moves.
- **`a->b`** — the group scenes. `the_common_room`, `courtyard_lunch_table`,
  `hallway_after_bell`, `annexe_two_of_them`. Taking a side costs something on an
  edge the player is not on.

## End-of-act state

A run that reaches the end of Act 1 has:

- `anchor_done(the_date)`, phase `odd`
- `knows(old_photo_exists)`, `knows(photo_dated_sept_32)`, `knows(ledger_column_undated)`
- somewhere between three and eight of the eleven other facts, depending
- a relationship graph with at least two characters above casual, and at least
  one edge the player damaged
- between zero and three commitments broken

What it does **not** have is a theory the game endorses. June's reading is intact
and defensible. So is Theo's. The player picks.

## Open questions Act 1 deliberately did not answer

- **What the number on the photograph's stamp actually is.** Written as "a
  number that is not a September date" wherever a character reads it aloud, so
  that whether it is `32` or something stranger stays open. `OPEN QUESTION`
- **Whether the fourteen names in the column are people who exist.** Nobody
  counts them out loud in Act 1. `OPEN QUESTION`
- **Where the bound volumes went.** The notice says the annexe. Nobody has
  checked. `OPEN QUESTION`
- **Why the school year the town describes skips.** Told third-hand by someone
  who says it was told to be entertaining. `OPEN QUESTION`

Each of these is a place Act 2 can go. None is a promise that it will.
