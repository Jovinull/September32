# Act 1 character arcs

One week per person. Not the whole game — nothing here settles a `core wound`,
all five of which remain `IDEA` in the bibles and must.

The test each of these has to pass: **the character wants something today**, and
it is not "solve September 32".

---

## Iris

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Starting state** | Has run the digitisation alone since March and has stopped noticing that this is unusual. |
| **Immediate want** | For the second index to be looked at properly by somebody who is not her. |
| **External problem** | Two hundred and six boxes, four hours a week, and a paper record that does not match what is in them. |
| **Internal contradiction** | Trusts photographs more than people, and is lonelier than anyone in the story. |
| **Wants from the player** | To be *accompanied*. Not believed — accompanied. |
| **Does NOT want from the player** | To be handled carefully, or told the second index is a good idea, or asked why it matters. |
| **Ordinary-life conflict** | She photographs things with no archival value at all — a drain cover, a shoe that keeps moving — and cannot say why, and is aware it is not interesting. |
| **Relationship possibilities** | The game's most available chemistry, and the most easily damaged: `trust` and `suspicion` both move fast around her, and telling Noah about the photograph costs her trust in a way nothing else in Act 1 does. |
| **Act 1 turning point** | `iris_the_second_box`. She has started it. Four cards, two hundred and six boxes, and she does not explain why. |
| **Changes by Day 7** | Somebody else has seen the shoebox. Whether they helped with it is the difference between two quite different Iris relationships. |
| **Stays hidden** | Why it matters to her. What she has filed and lost. Whether she has ever done it before. |

**The audit's finding.** Every Iris scene happened in or about the archive,
which made her the girl from the photograph rather than a person.
`iris_the_wrong_photograph` is the correction and is deliberately about nothing.

## Mara

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Starting state** | Third generation here. Was at the gate on a Sunday because somebody always is and it is usually her. |
| **Immediate want** | For the town to be understood correctly by an outsider, and to not be asked to perform it. |
| **External problem** | Everybody assumes she will do Thursday, and she does, and nobody has thanked her for four years. |
| **Internal contradiction** | Would defend this place to anyone, and has been counting down to leaving since she was fourteen. |
| **Wants from the player** | Permission to be from somewhere without being of it. |
| **Does NOT want from the player** | Outsiders being clever about the town. She goes quiet, and the quiet is the last warning. |
| **Ordinary-life conflict** | The rota, the kettle, and being the one who is always around — a role she was handed and has never examined. |
| **Relationship possibilities** | The one character the player's *own* view of visibly changes (`player->mara`), on a walk about a dog. |
| **Act 1 turning point** | `mara_is_wrong`. She reads a situation confidently, specifically, out of two real observations, and is wrong. |
| **Changes by Day 7** | Either somebody has corrected her kindly and she has filed it, or nobody has and she does not know. |
| **Stays hidden** | Why she stays. What her family handles by not mentioning. |

**The three things her bible separates** — what Mara knows, what she thinks she
knows, what she says she knows — had never come apart on screen. Every scene
showed her being right, which turns judgement into a mechanic. `mara_is_wrong`
is the correction, and the player chooses whether she ever finds out.

## Theo

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Starting state** | Keeps the digitisation spreadsheet and re-checks every box against the ledger. Nobody asked him to. |
| **Immediate want** | Column J full, and to not be told he is overthinking it. |
| **External problem** | Eleven boxes he cannot find, and a brass key signed out to him in March that he has lost. |
| **Internal contradiction** | Trusts the system entirely and is the only person methodically looking for where it fails. |
| **Wants from the player** | To be wrong about something small, safely, in front of somebody who does not make it a lesson. |
| **Does NOT want from the player** | To be told a script could do his job in a weekend. That is the one that lands. |
| **Ordinary-life conflict** | He keeps score at a television quiz, in a notebook, with other people's names in a column, and always loses round four. |
| **Relationship possibilities** | `respect` is cheap from him and means nothing; `familiarity` is how he actually indicates anything. He hands out proximity long before he hands out trust. |
| **Act 1 turning point** | `theo_admits_it`. He marked a box `seen` that he had not seen, which is the exact thing the column exists to prevent. |
| **Changes by Day 7** | Either somebody let him be wrong about something small, or somebody repeated his own criticism back at him, which is the only branch in Act 1 that lowers a trust axis. |
| **Stays hidden** | What was filed wrongly before, and who it cost. |

**The audit's finding.** He had method and no ego. A rationalist with no vanity
is an exposition device. `theo_the_quiz` gives him something he is competitive
about that does not matter, and the embarrassment of being caught at it.

## June

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Starting state** | Transferred in alone, in February, two years ago. The easiest person here to talk to. |
| **Immediate want** | For a birthday nobody noticed to get noticed, and for a phone call at half eleven to be four o'clock's problem. |
| **External problem** | She is the person who organises things, and it is now assumed. |
| **Internal contradiction** | The most sociable person in the building and the only one who arrived on their own. |
| **Wants from the player** | For it to be safe to be interested in something. |
| **Does NOT want from the player** | To be told she is in denial. She will agree pleasantly and leave. |
| **Ordinary-life conflict** | A bad afternoon, fully managed, with her phone face down beside her. |
| **Relationship possibilities** | The only character the player can watch deflect *in real time* and choose not to push. `player->june.respect` is earned by watching her be good at something. |
| **Act 1 turning point** | `june_reads_it_flat`. She argues the ordinary explanation and the argument is genuinely good, and rule R5 says the player must never feel stupid for agreeing with it. |
| **Changes by Day 7** | Either she has been let off a story she was using as cover, or she has closed a door so smoothly it takes until evening to notice which one. |
| **Stays hidden** | What she transferred away from. |

**The audit's finding.** Three of her four scenes showed her handling something
for somebody else. `june_is_not_fine` puts the player where they can notice
being deflected, without ever naming what she is deflecting from.

## Noah

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Starting state** | Runs a four-page paper that is largely him. |
| **Immediate want** | Eight centimetres for the third column, and twenty minutes in the archive with a camera. |
| **External problem** | Iris keeps saying not yet, and he has now asked five times. |
| **Internal contradiction** | Believes the truth belongs to everyone, and wants to be the one who tells it. |
| **Wants from the player** | Access, and — separately, and he would not put it this way — help. |
| **Does NOT want from the player** | To be told a story is not his to tell. |
| **Ordinary-life conflict** | Two hundred words on a fixture he did not watch, done properly; and a phone call from home on a Tuesday that he does not always want. |
| **Relationship possibilities** | `dependency`, which almost nobody else has. He notices being helped and says so lightly and does not mean it lightly. |
| **Act 1 turning point** | `annexe_two_of_them`. He and Iris are both right about something and the player has to pick. |
| **Changes by Day 7** | The piece is written or it is not, and whichever it is, somebody's trust moved. |
| **Stays hidden** | Why it matters that it be him. What the Tuesday call is about. |

**All three readings must survive Act 1** — he knows something, he is avoiding
something unrelated, he simply has an unusual personality — and the evidence for
the second was previously its absence. `noah_takes_a_call` supplies it and
refuses to resolve it: the call is about home, the player learns nothing
specific, and pressing him costs suspicion in both directions.

## Ms. Ellery

Not a lead, and should not become one. Her function was undecided; it is now
decided.

**What she contributes that no student can:** a wall made entirely of reasonable
things.

She refuses, correctly, with her reasoning fully on the page, and cannot be
argued out of it because there is nothing wrong with the reasoning. Every
student in the cast can be persuaded, worn down, or gone around. She cannot, and
she is not hostile, and she is not hiding anything. That texture is unavailable
from anybody else in the game and it is what an institution feels like from
inside.

Canon is explicit that she is not a conspiracy. Both her scenes hold that: she
signs the sheet, she explains the circularity, she is bothered by it on
Thursdays for about ten minutes.

**Two scenes is the right amount.** She is a boundary condition, and boundary
conditions stop meaning anything if they turn up constantly.
