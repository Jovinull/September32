# Act 1 mystery ledger

Everything odd the first week can put in front of a player, and what each thing
is and is not evidence of.

The rule this file enforces: **evidence is not interpretation.** Every entry
separates what objectively exists from what a player may infer, and every entry
has an ordinary explanation that is allowed to be the right one.

`SEPTEMBER_32.md` is still `IDEA` about almost everything. Nothing here narrows
it, and no scene may.

---

## E1 — The column with no date

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Objectively exists** | Attendance ledger vol. III, page 40 verso. A column ruled top and bottom, fourteen entries, all present marks, in the same hand as the columns either side. No date written above it. The columns before and after are the 15th and the 17th. |
| **Who can discover it** | Anyone. `the_undated_column` is an anchor and fires in 100% of runs; `theo_ledger_question` shows it earlier to about five runs in six. |
| **Player may infer** | That somebody wrote a register on a day and did not number it. |
| **Ordinary explanation** | Somebody was interrupted and came back to it after the moment when you write the date. Iris says exactly this, in the scene, and is probably right. June's version is better: nobody writes the date for themselves, you write it for the person in forty years. |
| **Strange explanation** | Undecided and must stay so. |
| **Does NOT prove** | That a day existed. That anybody was present. That fourteen is a meaningful number — **nobody counts the names out loud in Act 1**, deliberately. |
| **Future payoff** | The names. `OPEN QUESTION`. |

**Phase**: this is what turns `normal` into `odd`, and it is deliberately the
weakest thing available. One thing that does not fit, singular, with an ordinary
explanation everyone reaches for.

## E2 — The stamp on the photograph

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Objectively exists** | An unlabelled class photograph, out of sequence, loose in box 17 under a bundle of sports-day programmes. Three rows, back row on a bench, forty-odd faces. Trimmed on one edge, by hand, not squarely. Stamped once in the corner. The stamp is a September date, and the number after the month is not a number September has. |
| **Who can discover it** | Anyone. `the_date` is an anchor. `old_photo_glimpse` gets about 98% of runs there first, at which point the stamp is glimpsed and *suspected* rather than read. |
| **Player may infer** | That the stamp is wrong, or that the photograph is. |
| **Ordinary explanation** | A franking machine has little wheels and the wheels turn. Somebody set one a click too far, stamped a photograph and went to lunch. The scene offers this as its first menu option and does not undercut it. |
| **Strange explanation** | Undecided. |
| **Does NOT prove** | Anything on its own. The scene is explicit that it covers the stamp and does not cover the column, and that the player now has two things rather than one. |
| **Future payoff** | The photograph itself, which does not exist as art. `ART_PRODUCTION_PLAN.md`, P0. |

**Nobody reads the number aloud.** Not the protagonist, not Iris, not Theo. What
it is stays open, because a character saying "thirty-two" would be the scene
deciding canon.

## E3 — The year the school history skips

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Objectively exists** | The welcome booklet has five pages of school history, one paragraph per year since the twenties. One year has no paragraph. |
| **Who can discover it** | `june_welcome_pack`, ~77% of runs. |
| **Player may infer** | That a year is missing from the record. |
| **Ordinary explanation** | They print forty a year on a machine older than the roof. Things fall off pages. June says this and *believes it*, and her relief at being agreed with is the interesting part of the scene rather than the gap. |
| **Does NOT prove** | Which year. June never knew — she noticed it at fifteen, on a Sunday, waiting for somebody to find her a key. |
| **Future payoff** | Corroboration only. |

## E4 — The missing run of the paper

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Objectively exists** | The bound volumes of the school paper are missing a stretch from the middle. About a year, near enough; Noah checks, because he checks. |
| **Who can discover it** | `noah_evening_layout`, ~79% of runs. |
| **Ordinary explanation** | Noah's own, and it is the strongest sceptical line in Act 1: *"It's a small school and it's had one bad flood and about six reorganisations. If you go looking for missing years you'll find missing years."* And then, unprompted: *"Doesn't mean there's nothing in it. Just means it's cheap evidence."* |
| **Does NOT prove** | That it is the same year as E3. Nobody has checked and nobody can. |
| **Red herring status** | **Deliberate.** E3 and E4 are two year-long gaps that a player will want to be the same gap, and the game will not confirm it. |

## E5 — The town's version

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Objectively exists** | There is a year the town did not print a yearbook. Mara's grandmother was here for it. There is a version of why, third-hand, from a woman who told it to be entertaining. |
| **Who can discover it** | `mara_older_story`, ~87% of runs. |
| **Player may infer** | Almost nothing, because she will not tell it. |
| **Ordinary explanation** | Mara supplies one herself, unasked, as a gift: somebody could have faked a stamp on a photograph. People did things for a laugh then the same as now. |
| **Does NOT prove** | Anything. She says so: *"That's not the version. That's just me being sensible at you."* |
| **Belief state** | Grants `photo_is_a_forgery` as `suspected`, **never** as `known`, and it contradicts `photo_dated_sept_32`. A player can end the week holding both, which is the point. |

## E6 — The bound volumes went to the annexe

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Objectively exists** | A laminated notice in the glass case by the gate records the transfer. A transfer card in the archive records what moved. The card has a field for who holds the key. It is blank. |
| **Who can discover it** | `gate_noticeboard` (~67%), then `annexe_transfer_card` in the viewer. |
| **Ordinary explanation** | A library was reorganised. Things went into storage. The notice outlived whoever was supposed to take it down. |
| **Does NOT prove** | That anything is being kept from anybody. A blank field is an absence, not a discovery. |

## E7 — The annexe is locked and nobody has the key

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Objectively exists** | A door at the end of the east corridor. Brass plate, painted over at the join twice, no scuff on the skirting. The office does not hold the key; it went with a maintenance contract. Theo was signed out a brass key in March and has lost it. |
| **Who can discover it** | `annexe_door` (~64%), `ellery_and_the_form`, `theo_admits_it` (~47%). |
| **Player may infer** | That there is a room nobody has opened in years. |
| **Ordinary explanation** | Ellery's, and it is airtight: nothing has left the archive since 2011, there is no movement log, and to start one somebody would have to know what is in the boxes, which is the job the player is doing. It is completely circular and it has been since before the player was born. |
| **Does NOT prove** | That anything is behind the door. `annexe_door` says so explicitly — the player learns one thing, which is that the door is real. |

## E8 — Iris keeps a second index

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Objectively exists** | A shoebox of handwritten cards. Box number, rough description, date range with a question mark. It does not match the accession record, and it never has. |
| **Who can discover it** | `archive_iris_index` (~46%). |
| **Ordinary explanation** | The accession record records the order things arrived in, not what they are. You cannot find anything with it. |
| **Does NOT prove** | Anything about the archive. It proves something about Iris, which is the point of it. |
| **Note** | This is the entry most likely to be *misread* as a clue. It is characterisation that happens to be about records. |

## The shape

| | Act 1 |
| --- | ---: |
| Things that objectively exist | 8 |
| With an ordinary explanation stated **by a character, in the scene** | 7 |
| That prove anything on their own | **0** |
| That the game confirms are connected | **0** |
| Marked `OPEN QUESTION` in `ACT_1.md` | 4 |

Two of the eight (E3 and E4) are year-long gaps that a player will want to be
the same gap. The game will not say. Two more (E6, E7) are a door and the
paperwork about a door, and the paperwork is complete and boring and the door
does not open.

**Questions grow faster than answers**, which is the Act 1 requirement. By Day 7
the player holds two records that do not agree and no account covering both, and
every single ordinary explanation they have been offered is still standing.

## What must not happen

- No character may connect two entries for the player. They may each notice one.
- No entry may be confirmed. `disprove()` is available and Act 1 uses it exactly
  once, on an NPC's stance, never on the player's.
- The player is never told their theory. There is no UI for it and there will
  not be.
- No new entry may be added to this ledger without a payoff act named in it.
  Seven of the eight above pay off in Act 2 or later; none is a loose thread
  planted for texture.
