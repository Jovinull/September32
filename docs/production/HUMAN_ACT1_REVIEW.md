# Human review queue — Act 1 draft 1

Thirteen scenes, in priority order, with what to look for in each.

Everything a tool can check has been checked: reachability, references,
continuity guards, relationship direction, choice-shape distribution, declared
outcomes, and the absence of the phrases that give AI prose away. **None of that
is evidence that any of it is good.**

What follows needs a person. The AI review has been completed first — this is
not a list of things that were skipped.

## How to use this

Read in the order given. It is roughly forty minutes of reading.

For each, the question is not "is this competent" — the prose is competent
throughout, which is exactly the failure mode of machine-written fiction. The
question is **"would I want to know this person by the end of the week?"**

Play or read the scene, then answer the specific question underneath it.

---

## 1. `mara_is_wrong` — the scene the milestone is proudest of

`game/story/storylets/act1_depth.rpy`

Mara reads a situation confidently, specifically, out of two real observations,
and is wrong. The player knows she is wrong. The three branches are: correct her
kindly, press her about what happens when she is wrong, or say nothing.

**Look for:** does the "say nothing" branch land as a *choice* or as an
omission? It is meant to be the one that costs the player something they will
carry without her ever knowing, and that is a hard thing to make felt.

**Also:** her bible says being contradicted about people is what she takes
worst. Does the gentle branch respect that, or does she take it too well?

---

## 2. `june_is_not_fine` — the deflection

`game/story/storylets/act1_depth.rpy`

June with her phone face down, telling a good story at slightly the wrong speed.
The player can let her off it, sit through it, or ask what is on the phone.

**Look for:** the third branch is meant to be the player watching a door close
so smoothly it takes until evening to notice which door. Does it read as that,
or does it read as June being fine and the narration insisting otherwise?

**This is the scene most at risk of being about nothing.** It is deliberately
about nothing — an ordinary bad afternoon — and the line between "understated"
and "empty" is exactly here.

---

## 3. `the_common_room` (anchor) — the best group scene

`game/story/anchors/anchors.rpy`

Four people. Rewritten this milestone to stop being round-robin: June is
interrupted mid-word, Theo contributes and is not acknowledged, Mara and Theo
conduct an entire exchange by moving a mug four inches.

**Look for:** does it sound like four people, or like one writer taking turns
with four labels? Cover the names on any ten consecutive lines and see whether
you can still tell.

**The mug.** It is one sentence and it is doing more work than anything else in
the scene. Does it survive being that quiet, or does it need a beat?

---

## 4. `theo_the_quiz` — is Theo likeable?

`game/story/storylets/act1_depth.rpy`

Theo keeping score at a television quiz, in a notebook, with other people's
names in a column from previous weeks, always losing round four.

**Look for:** the notebook has four names in it and three of them have stopped
playing, and he does not say the thing, and it is very obvious what the thing
is. **Is it too obvious?** This is the milestone's most sentimental scene and
the one most likely to have overreached.

---

## 5. `iris_the_second_box` — three relationship states

`game/story/storylets/act1_together.rpy`

The scene reads `stance("iris")` and plays differently at `wary`, at high
attraction, and in the ordinary case.

**Look for:** the `wary` branch is meant to be *the same invitation, extended by
somebody who decided to extend it anyway* — not a colder version. Read all three
openings back to back. Is `wary` a third thing, or is it just less warm?

**How to reach it:** tell Noah about the photograph (`noah_asks_for_access`,
third option), then reach this scene.

---

## 6. `annexe_two_of_them` — the sharpest fork

`game/story/storylets/act1_together.rpy`

Noah wants to print a piece about the archive; Iris will not have it. The player
picks, and the two NPC-to-NPC axes move in opposite directions.

**Look for:** are both of them right about something? The scene's whole claim is
that neither position is the wrong one, and if a reader finishes it knowing whom
they were supposed to side with, it has failed.

**The third branch** does not make either of them like the player more, and
leaves the two of them marginally easier with each other. Is that satisfying or
is it a cop-out?

---

## 7. `the_date` (anchor) — the Day 7 hook

`game/story/anchors/anchors.rpy`

The end of the act. The stamp, read twice.

**Look for:** does it make you want to play Day 8? Not "is it creepy" — the
target is curiosity rather than unease. The three menu options are a franking
machine set wrong, the column and the stamp not covering each other, and putting
it back where it was.

**Specifically:** the scene is careful that the stamp alone has an ordinary
explanation and that the *pair* of documents does not. Does that land, or does
it read as the game hedging?

## 8. `courtyard_small_talk` — the most-played scene in the game

`game/story/storylets/prototype_storylets.rpy`

Played about five times a run. Rewritten this milestone to read which characters
the player has got close to, and hand their week back to them.

**Look for:** it will be read five times. Does the third reading still work?
Does the version where the player has nobody yet read as lonely or as empty?

## 9. `mara_the_long_way` — the most romantic scene that is not a romance

`game/story/storylets/act1_people.rpy`

A walk, a wired-shut gate belonging to a family that no longer exists, and a dog
called Bill who will stop barking at you in about a year.

**Look for:** "You'll come past and he won't get up, and you'll feel worse about
it than you expect to." That is the line the whole scene is built on. Does it
earn its place, or is it the writing admiring itself?

---

## 10. `june_reads_it_flat` — rule R5

`game/story/storylets/act1_week.rpy`

June argues that the undated column is somebody's Tuesday, and the argument is
good.

**Look for:** the canonical requirement is that a player must never feel stupid
for agreeing with her. Read the sceptical branch. **Does agreeing feel
intelligent, or does it feel like the option the game is waiting for you to
abandon?** If the second, R5 is not actually being honoured and the whole
mystery structure is weaker than it looks.

---

## 11. `ellery_and_the_form` — the adult

`game/story/storylets/act1_depth.rpy`

Ms. Ellery refuses something small, correctly, with her whole reasoning on the
page, and cannot be argued out of it.

**Look for:** she is played off-sprite, as a voice and a folder, because there
is no sprite. **Does that read as a stylistic choice or as an absence?** The
answer determines whether the character-art brief is P1 or P0.

---

## 12. `noah_takes_a_call` — the scene I am least confident about

`game/story/storylets/act1_depth.rpy`

Noah steps away for a phone call from home and comes back putting the phone in
the wrong pocket.

**Look for:** this exists to keep alive the reading that Noah is avoiding
something *unrelated* to the mystery. The risk is that a phone call he does not
want to take reads as a clue no matter how ordinary the writing makes it — in
which case the scene achieves the opposite of its purpose and should be cut.

**Ask honestly:** after reading it, do you suspect Noah more or less?

---

## 13. `theo_admits_it`, third branch — the only cruelty

`game/story/storylets/act1_together.rpy`

The player repeats Theo's own self-criticism back at him. It is the only branch
in Act 1 that lowers a trust axis.

**Look for:** does it read as a real option somebody might pick, or as a trap
the game is daring you into? A single available cruelty in a whole act is either
very effective or conspicuous, and I cannot tell which.

## Candidate memorable moments

If Act 1 works, a player should be able to name three specific moments
afterwards. These are the candidates, and whether any of them are actually
memorable is precisely what a machine cannot assess.

1. Mara making three cups of tea without asking anybody, and Theo moving his
   four inches to the left without looking up.
2. Iris taking the paperweight out of your hands and apologising while she does
   it, and meaning the apology and not the taking.
3. Bill the dog, and the year it takes him to stop.
4. Theo's notebook with four names in it and three of them gone.
5. June's four-minute plan for a birthday nobody else noticed.
6. The bench in the back row of the photograph being the same bench that is
   still in the courtyard.
7. "There is famously a bus."

## What was NOT reviewed by a person, and should not be assumed good

- Whether any of the jokes are funny. Humour was audited for *sameness* — the
  five characters joke in measurably different registers — and that is a
  different claim from any of them landing.
- Whether the romance reads as chemistry or as a variable going up.
- Whether the week is paced well as an experience rather than as a distribution.
- Whether the protagonist's narration is enjoyable to spend 12,700 words with.
  It is 68% of the game's prose and it had no bible until this milestone.
