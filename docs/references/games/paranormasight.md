# PARANORMASIGHT

Original analysis. No content from the work is reproduced here.

## USE FOR

**Rules that the player learns and can reason with.** Its supernatural has
mechanics, stated plainly, and the tension comes from applying them.

**Conflicting objectives.** Multiple people want incompatible things, all
sympathetically.

**Urban legend as texture.** Something a place tells about itself, not an
explanation.

## DO NOT COPY

The curses, the setting, any character, the specific rule set, the meta
elements.

## PRINCIPLE

> Give the supernatural rules the player can hold, and the fear becomes
> anticipation rather than confusion.

## RELEVANCE TO SEPTEMBER 32

`docs/canon/PHENOMENON_RULES.md` is this principle applied at the design level.
The rules are constraints on what any eventual explanation may be, written down
*before* the explanation is chosen, so that scenes can be written now without
being retracted later.

Rule R4 in particular — the phenomenon never performs — is a rule the player can
learn to rely on, which makes every discrepancy in a document more frightening
rather than less.

Conflicting objectives maps onto Noah: he wants to publish, which is
incompatible with Iris's caution, and neither is wrong.

## RISKS

**Explaining too early.** Its rules are stated up front because it is a puzzle
game. This is not; the constraints are for the *writers*, and the player learns
them by observation.
