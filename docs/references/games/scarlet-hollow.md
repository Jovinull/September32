# Scarlet Hollow

Original analysis. No content from the work is reproduced here.

## USE FOR

**Traits that change what you can see.** A trait gates an *additional* option —
a way of perceiving — rather than a better outcome. A player without it never
feels punished.

**Relationships that are not one axis.** Characters can like and distrust you
simultaneously, and the game writes that difference.

**Branches that recombine.** Divergence without permanently splitting the content
tree.

**Horror grounded in a place and its people.**

## DO NOT COPY

The setting, the trait list, any character, the specific supernatural elements.

## PRINCIPLE

> A trait should change what the player notices, not how well they do.

## RELEVANCE TO SEPTEMBER 32

The trait system is built to this shape. Traits are accumulated, never chosen;
the threshold is low (3); and a trait-gated option is always additional and
always labelled `[Observant]` so the player can see they earned it
(`docs/narrative-design/CHOICES.md`).

The eight relationship axes exist for the same reason its relationships are not
one number: `love without trust` has to be representable, and there is a test
asserting it is.

## RISKS

**Trait bloat.** Eight axes is already a lot. A ninth needs to justify itself
against the seven scenes it will not appear in.

**Traits as a build.** If a player optimises for a trait, it has become a
character sheet. Keeping thresholds low and rewards frequent-but-small is what
prevents it.
