# Slay the Princess

Original analysis. No content from the work is reproduced here.

## USE FOR

**Choices that change the protagonist**, not just the world. Who you have been
alters who you are, and the game shows the alteration.

**Recontextualisation as structure.** Returning to a situation that is
recognisably the same and entirely different.

**Perception as unreliable, made explicit** — with the narrator as a character
who can be argued with.

## DO NOT COPY

The premise, the structure, the voices, the loop, any character, the tone.

## PRINCIPLE

> A choice can change the person making the next one. Say so, visibly, and the
> player starts choosing differently.

## RELEVANCE TO SEPTEMBER 32

The traits system is the mechanism. Accumulated tendencies alter what options
appear, so the protagonist a player has been shapes the protagonist they can be.

Whether to make that legible — a screen showing accumulated tendencies — is open.
The argument for is that visible change is what makes it land; the argument
against is that it invites optimisation.

## RISKS

**Its degree of transformation** requires its premise. This game's protagonist is
a student with a filing job, and the same technique at that intensity would be
absurd.

**Narrator as character.** Ruled out early — see `docs/narrative-design/META.md`.
The narration here is close third, and it does not have opinions about the
player.
