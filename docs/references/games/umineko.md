# Umineko When They Cry

Original analysis. No content from the work is reproduced here.

## USE FOR

**The gap between what happened and what is claimed.** It treats narration as
testimony rather than as fact, and makes the reader responsible for the
difference.

**Evidence with rules.** Rather than "trust nothing", it establishes what kinds
of claim are reliable and under what conditions. Constraint is what makes
ambiguity productive instead of exhausting.

**Reinterpretation of shown material.** The same events, re-read, meaning
something else — without retconning. Everything was there.

**Emotional truth against factual truth**, treated as a real tension rather than
a trick.

## DO NOT COPY

The Sea Fragments, the game-board conceit, any character, the specific mystery,
the fantasy framing, its terminology.

## PRINCIPLE

> If narration can be unreliable, the reader must be given rules for judging it.
> Ambiguity without rules is noise.

## RELEVANCE TO SEPTEMBER 32

This is why the knowledge model has five stances rather than a boolean.
`false_belief` is the mechanism: a character can be confidently wrong, and the
player can be too, and the engine tracks both separately
(`docs/style/MYSTERY.md`).

Rule R2 — nothing is proven by a single document — is a rule for judging
evidence, given to the player rather than reserved by the author. That is the
lesson.

The contradiction tracker (`s32_engine.knowledge.contradictions`) exists so that
mutually exclusive evidence is a *tracked state* rather than an authorial trick.

## RISKS

**Ambiguity as an excuse.** Its ambiguity is rigorous; imitating the ambiguity
without the rigour produces a story that declined to conclude. Rule R3 helps:
nobody has the full picture, but the game must eventually show whether a
confident character was mistaken or lying.

**Scale.** It is enormous. Its structural devices assume that length.
