# Zero Escape

Original analysis. No content from the work is reproduced here.

## USE FOR

**Knowledge as the actual key.** Progress gated on what the *player* knows, not
on what the character has. Replaying is not repetition; it is how information is
gathered.

**A visible flow.** The player can see the structure of what they have explored
and what remains, which makes replaying feel like investigation rather than
grinding.

**Endings as information.** Reaching one is not failure; it is a source.

## DO NOT COPY

The escape rooms. The specific meta-conceit and its explanation. Any character.
The specific flowchart implementation.

## PRINCIPLE

> If the player is going to replay, make replaying a mechanic rather than a
> concession — and show them the shape of what they have not seen.

## RELEVANCE TO SEPTEMBER 32

`docs/narrative-design/META.md` takes the *player-knowledge* half and rejects the
*character-knowledge* half. Rule R7: the protagonist does not remember; the
player does. The rare `[You remember.]` option is the visible expression.

The flowchart idea is present as a **developer** tool only — `s32 graph` exports
the information graph. Whether the player ever sees a structure like it is open
(`docs/canon/OPEN_QUESTIONS.md`); showing it would give away the shape of the
mystery.

## RISKS

**Requiring replays.** Its structure makes multiple playthroughs mandatory. This
game must be complete in one, with replaying offering perspective rather than
completion.

**Meta as plot.** Its cross-run knowledge is explained in-fiction. Doing that here
would mean deciding what September 32 is, in order to justify a UI convenience.
