# The Great God Pan

**Arthur Machen, 1894.** Author died 1947.

**Public-domain basis:** published before 1930 (public domain in the United
States), and more than 70 years after the author's death (public domain in
life+70 jurisdictions). Verified 2026-08-30 — see
`docs/references/literature/PUBLIC_DOMAIN.md` for the corpus policy.

Original analysis. No text from the work is reproduced here.

## USE FOR

**A mystery assembled from documents and accounts.** Letters, testimony,
newspaper items, conversations — the reader assembles what the characters cannot.

**Separate events that turn out to be one event.** Each is explicable alone.

**Investigators comparing notes**, which is the actual pleasure of the form.

**The horror mostly off-page**, reported rather than shown.

## DO NOT COPY

The premise, the experiment, the characters, the specific chain of events, the
mythological framing.

## PRINCIPLE

> Give the reader the documents and let them do the connecting. The moment they
> assemble it themselves is worth more than any reveal.

## RELEVANCE TO SEPTEMBER 32

This is the structural model for the archive plot. Separate items — a
photograph, a ledger column, a run of the student paper with a gap — each with an
ordinary explanation, which the player connects before any character does.

Two people comparing notes is literally the `theo_ledger_question` scene, and it
should be the shape of most investigation content: not discovery, but
corroboration.

## RISKS

**Assuming the connection is obvious.** In a branching game the player may not
have seen all the pieces. The knowledge model exists partly so the game can *know*
what they have, and offer the connecting scene only when they can make it.
