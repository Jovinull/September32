# The King in Yellow

**Robert W. Chambers, 1895.** Author died 1933.

**Public-domain basis:** published before 1930 (public domain in the United
States), and more than 70 years after the author's death (public domain in
life+70 jurisdictions). Verified 2026-08-30 — see
`docs/references/literature/PUBLIC_DOMAIN.md` for the corpus policy.

Original analysis. No text from the work is reproduced here.

## USE FOR

**A document that damages the reader.** The book within the book is dangerous
not because of what it does but because of what it *says*, and the stories never
show enough of it to break the spell.

**Knowledge as contagion.** Having read it is a state a character is in.

**Recurring motifs as connective tissue** between stories that share no plot. A
phrase, a symbol, a name — repetition doing the work that continuity usually
does.

**Withholding by design.** The play is never quoted at length. Every attempt to
show more would reduce it.

## DO NOT COPY

The Yellow Sign, Carcosa, Hastur, the play itself, its terminology, any
character or story. Much of this vocabulary has been so widely borrowed that
using it now reads as a genre signal rather than a reference.

## PRINCIPLE

> A dangerous document is most dangerous unread. Show its effects; withhold its
> contents.

## RELEVANCE TO SEPTEMBER 32

The archive is a room full of documents, and the temptation will be to show the
player exactly what each one says. This is the argument for restraint: the
photograph's stamp is described as a date ending in 32 long before the player
reads the whole thing, and the withholding is what makes it work
(`docs/style/MYSTERY.md` — withhold the specific, not the general).

The motif idea is also directly useful: recurring details across unconnected
storylets — a green cord, a corrected photocopy, the same handwriting — connect
scenes that share no plot.

## RISKS

**Borrowed vocabulary.** Its terms are so widespread that any use reads as
pastiche.

**Danger as spectacle.** Rule R4: the phenomenon never performs. A document that
visibly harms someone would break it.
