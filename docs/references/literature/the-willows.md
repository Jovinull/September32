# The Willows

**Algernon Blackwood, 1907.** Author died 1951.

**Public-domain basis:** published before 1930 (public domain in the United
States), and more than 70 years after the author's death (public domain in
life+70 jurisdictions). Verified 2026-08-30 — see
`docs/references/literature/PUBLIC_DOMAIN.md` for the corpus policy.

Original analysis. No text from the work is reproduced here.

## USE FOR

**A familiar place becoming wrong by degrees.** Nothing arrives. The place
changes, slowly, and the characters keep finding ordinary explanations slightly
past the point where they hold.

**A phenomenon that resists categorisation.** It is never named, never explained,
and never given a shape — and that is why it works.

**Two people talking themselves out of it**, which is both realistic and the
source of the dread.

**Accumulated small wrongness.** Sand shifting. A shape. A sound. Individually
nothing.

## DO NOT COPY

The river, the island, the willows, the specific entities.

## PRINCIPLE

> Let the characters reach for ordinary explanations slightly longer than the
> reader can. The gap is where the dread lives.

## RELEVANCE TO SEPTEMBER 32

The closest match in tone to what this game wants. Rules R2 and R4 together are
essentially Blackwood's method: nothing is proven by a single observation, and the
phenomenon never performs.

The "talking themselves out of it" dynamic is exactly the Theo/June/Iris triangle
— three people finding three different ordinary explanations, all reasonable, for
slightly too long.

## RISKS

**Its length and stillness.** A short story can sustain atmosphere with almost no
plot. A game with a day cycle needs things to *do*, and the ambient storylets are
where that texture has to live without becoming filler.
