# The Turn of the Screw

**Henry James, 1898.** Author died 1916.

**Public-domain basis:** published before 1930 (public domain in the United
States), and more than 70 years after the author's death (public domain in
life+70 jurisdictions). Verified 2026-08-30 — see
`docs/references/literature/PUBLIC_DOMAIN.md` for the corpus policy.

Original analysis. No text from the work is reproduced here.

## USE FOR

**Ambiguity sustained to the end and never resolved**, in a way that reads as
deliberate rather than evasive. Both readings are fully supported by the text.

**A frame narrative that distances the account** — this is a story someone is
telling about a manuscript someone else wrote.

**Class and institution as pressure** on what can be said aloud.

**Restraint about what is shown.** The apparitions are described plainly and
briefly.

## DO NOT COPY

The governess, the children, the house, the specific ambiguity, the frame.

## PRINCIPLE

> Ambiguity is only satisfying when both readings are fully supported. If one is
> obviously correct, the ambiguity is decoration.

## RELEVANCE TO SEPTEMBER 32

Rule R5: an ordinary explanation must stay available and the sceptical player
must never feel stupid. That is this principle stated as a design constraint, and
June is the character who makes it true.

Getting it right means writing the mundane reading *well* — as well as the
supernatural one — which is more work than it sounds and is where most stories in
this mode fail.

## RISKS

**Ambiguity that is really indecision.** The difference is whether the author
knows. `docs/canon/SEPTEMBER_32.md` keeps the explanation open on purpose, but
`PHENOMENON_RULES.md` exists so that openness is constrained rather than
unresolved.
