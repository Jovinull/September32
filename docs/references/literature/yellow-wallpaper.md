# The Yellow Wallpaper

**Charlotte Perkins Gilman, 1892.** Author died 1935.

**Public-domain basis:** published before 1930 (public domain in the United
States), and more than 70 years after the author's death (public domain in
life+70 jurisdictions). Verified 2026-08-30 — see
`docs/references/literature/PUBLIC_DOMAIN.md` for the corpus policy.

Original analysis. No text from the work is reproduced here.

## USE FOR

**A narrator whose reliability degrades in front of the reader**, without ever
announcing it. The prose stays lucid; only the content drifts.

**Obsessive attention as characterisation.** What a person notices, and how much,
is who they are.

**Confinement making a small space enormous.** One room, described repeatedly,
becoming a world.

**Institutional dismissal.** Being told you are imagining it, by someone who
means well.

## DO NOT COPY

The wallpaper, the room, the narrator, the specific medical framing.

## PRINCIPLE

> Let the narration stay calm while what it describes stops making sense. The
> reader notices before the narrator does.

## RELEVANCE TO SEPTEMBER 32

Iris's second index is obsessive attention as characterisation — nine thousand
photographs of nothing, which is either a quirk or the most important fact in the
story.

The dismissal principle is what June represents from the inside and what Ms.
Ellery may represent institutionally: being told, kindly, that there is nothing
here.

The narration technique is the one `docs/style/HORROR.md` calls "report; do not
instruct". The prose never says something is wrong.

## RISKS

**Unreliability as a twist.** Its unreliability is a condition, not a reveal.
"None of it was real" is explicitly ruled out in `docs/style/MYSTERY.md`.

**Its subject matter.** Not this game's to borrow.
