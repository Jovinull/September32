# Horror

The horror in September 32 is **archival**. It is a discrepancy in a record, and
everything frightening about it is inferential. Rule R4 says the phenomenon never
performs — it does not manifest, appear, chase, or address anyone.

That constraint is not a limitation. It is the entire effect.

## What works here

**Inconsistency.** Two documents that cannot both be right. The horror is not
either document; it is the reader's growing certainty that one of them is a lie
and no way to tell which.

**Familiarity, broken slightly.** The room the player has been in eight times,
with one thing different. This only works if they have been in it eight times —
which is what the `normal` phase is buying.

**Doubt about one's own attention.** Not "did I imagine it" as a stated question,
but the reader supplying it themselves because the prose declined to.

**Silence.** A character who does not answer. A room described without a sound in
it. A block of narration that is one short sentence and then the scene moves on.

**Detail that is too specific.** "Fourteen entries" is more unsettling than "a
lot of entries". Precision implies someone was counting, and someone counting
implies someone knew.

**Anticipation over event.** The player's model of what might be true, allowed to
run ahead of what has been shown. Almost everything frightening in the finished
game should happen in the player's head.

**Recontextualisation.** A scene from three hours ago, meaning something else now.
This is the most powerful tool available and it requires setups nobody flagged as
setups.

## What does not work here

**Jump scares.** Nothing in this game's toolkit supports one, and the attempt
would cash in the accumulated unease for a second of adrenaline.

**Glitch aesthetics.** Corrupted text, flickering, distorted sprites. It has
become shorthand — the player reads "glitch" as "the game is doing the horror
part now" and stops being unsettled.

**Black eyes. Blood without function. A face at a window.** Borrowed imagery
carries its source's genre with it.

**Telling the reader.** "Something was wrong." "A chill went through you." "You
felt watched." Every one of these converts a reader who was noticing into a
reader being instructed.

**The phenomenon doing something.** R4. If a scene has it act, the scene is
wrong.

**Early meta.** See `docs/narrative-design/META.md`.

## The temperature

Counter-intuitively, the prose gets **plainer** as the game gets more frightening.

`normal` — warm, funny, unhurried.
`odd` — the same, with one sentence that does not quite fit and is not remarked
on.
`unsettling` — shorter paragraphs. Fewer jokes, and the jokes that remain are
someone deflecting.
`impossible` — flat, declarative, procedural. The prose describes what is there
and declines to interpret.
`consequences` — warmth returns, changed.

A writer's instinct at the `impossible` phase is to reach for atmosphere.
Resisting that instinct is most of the craft here.

## A calibration

The most frightening line in the prototype:

> You read the stamp twice, which is once more than a date needs.

No adjectives. No atmosphere. It describes an action and makes one dry
observation, and the reader does the rest. That is the target.
