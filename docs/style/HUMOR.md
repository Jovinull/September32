# Humour

The game is funnier than its premise suggests, and that is deliberate. The
warmth of the first phase is what the later phases spend.

## The register

**Dry.** Observational, understated, delivered without a pause for the laugh.

**Character-specific.** Each person is funny in a way that is also information
about them:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Iris | Self-directed, used to move past something. |
| Theo | Deadpan; a precise observation delivered flat. |
| Mara | Very dry, easy to miss, mostly about the town. |
| June | Frequent, generous — and, when deflecting, the tell. |
| Noah | Enthusiastic, occasionally at someone's expense, then apologetic. |

**Affectionate.** The characters like each other. The jokes are between people
who are pleased to be in the same room.

**Structural, often.** The funniest thing in the prototype is a gate that says
Northfield with the F missing, and nobody has fixed it, and at this point nobody
would. No character delivers it. It is just true.

## What to avoid

**Announcing the joke.** A joke followed by a character reacting to the joke is
one beat too many.

**Quips in tense scenes.** A joke that undercuts a moment costs more than it
earns. June deflecting with humour is different — that is characterisation, and
the reader is meant to notice the deflection.

**Humour at a character's expense from the narration.** The narration likes
everyone in this game.

**Reference humour.** It dates, and it does not translate.

**Being funny during the horror.** The `impossible` phase is not funny. That
contrast is a resource, and spending it early leaves the late game with nothing
to be.

## Where humour belongs

- Ambient and slice-of-life storylets, freely.
- First meetings, where it establishes register.
- Narration about the physical world — vending machines, crows, a brick.
- Deflection, when a character does not want to answer.

## Where it does not

- Immediately after something has landed.
- Anywhere in the `impossible` phase.
- In a document. The archive is not funny; the archive is flat, and its flatness
  is the joke and also the horror.

## A calibration

> The vending machine takes your coin and thinks about it. You have started to
> find this endearing, which is a bad sign about the week.

Two sentences, one observation, and a small piece of characterisation smuggled in
at the end. Nobody is performing. That is the target.
